## 💡 Key Discoveries

### 1. Lighting Affects Camera FPS (Hardware Behavior)

**Discovery**: Initial 16.7 FPS was due to low ambient lighting, not software issue

**Controlled Test Results**:
| Lighting | FPS | Explanation |
|----------|-----|-------------|
| Desk lamp ON | 27-29 FPS | Optimal performance ✅ |
| Desk lamp OFF | 16-17 FPS | Camera auto-throttle for better exposure ⚠️ |

**Conclusion**: This is **normal camera hardware behavior**, not a software problem. Camera automatically reduces FPS in low light to increase exposure time for better image quality.

### 2. Pipeline Decoupling Works

**Camera Pipeline** (Independent):
- Runs at 27-29 FPS
- YUV Conversion: 4.6ms avg
- Transform: 18.3ms avg
- Total: ~23ms (supports 27 FPS) ✅

**Analysis Pipeline** (Throttled):
- Throttled to 10 FPS via `.sample(100)`
- ML Kit: ~120ms avg
- Lighting: ~28ms avg
- Total E2E: ~140ms (doesn't affect camera) ✅

**Key Insight**: ML Kit's 120ms processing doesn't block camera FPS because:
1. Runs on background thread (`analysisExecutor`)
2. Throttled to 10 FPS (independent of camera)
3. Completely decoupled pipeline architecture

### 3. Quick Win > Plan B

**Decision**: Skip RenderScript YUV+Transform fusion (Plan B)

**Reasoning**:
- ✅ FPS target already achieved (27.2 > 26)
- ❌ Plan B adds high complexity
- ❌ Risky (potential regressions)
- ❌ Limited ROI (1-2 FPS gain not worth risk)
- ✅ Quick Win provides clean, minimal solution
